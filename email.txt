We are a mid-sized cargo airline. We carry goods for business customers on scheduled flights between a handful of
airports. Our customers range from steel manufacturers and auto-parts suppliers to pharmaceutical companies. Some ship
with us every week, others only now and then, and their shipments vary a lot: a single crate, a few boxes, or a large
piece of heavy machinery.

Customers book each shipment in advance through our booking system and then drop the goods off at our cargo terminal.
From that moment, the shipment waits until a flight can take it. On busy weeks, some cargo can sit in our hangar for
days before a flight finally picks it up. The booking system keeps a record of every shipment. The planners tell me the
records are generally good, although no system is perfect.

A few hours before each departure, one of our cargo planners reviews everything waiting for that flight and prepares a
loading proposal. On quiet days everything fits and the job is easy. On busy days, which are becoming more common, there
is more cargo waiting than the aircraft can carry, and the planner has to decide which cargo goes on this flight. Once
the proposal is approved, the ground team uses it to prepare the cargo and load the aircraft.

Today, planners build these proposals from the booking information, a spreadsheet and years of experience. Our most
experienced planners are very good at it, but it takes time, and two planners looking at the same flight do not always
reach the same answer. When a new planner joins, it takes months before they feel confident. And mistakes matter: a poor
plan can cost us money, and some mistakes are far more serious than that.

Our shipments are not all alike, and neither are our customers. Some customers are more important to us than others,
whether because of the business they bring, how long they have been with us, or what they ship, and our planners know
who they are. I have heard that the planners would like a tool that picks the best shipments for each flight, and that
sounds right to me. The tool should help us make good use of every aircraft and always respect our operating limits. It
should not replace the planner. Planners know things a computer does not, so they must be able to review the proposal
and make the final call.

We know a tool like this could eventually do many things. For now, we would like to start with something simple that
works, and improve it from there.

I am not a planner myself, so I may have missed important details. Please talk to the planner directly to find out what
they really need.
